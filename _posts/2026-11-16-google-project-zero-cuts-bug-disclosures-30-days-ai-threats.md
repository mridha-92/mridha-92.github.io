### Executive Summary

Google Project Zero has announced a policy shift replacing its traditional "90+30" day vulnerability disclosure model with shorter, fixed deadlines. The policy update aims to address the evolving security landscape, specifically responding to threat actors leveraging artificial intelligence for accelerated vulnerability discovery and exploitation.

Under the updated framework, vendors will face reduced windows to patch reported flaws before public disclosure. This shift underscores a growing industry need to accelerate patch deployment timelines to stay ahead of automated exploitation capabilities.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The primary driver behind this policy change is the escalation of AI-assisted threat capabilities. Adversaries and security researchers alike are increasingly utilizing AI tools to automate vulnerability analysis, reverse-engineering, and exploit generation, drastically reducing the time between vulnerability identification and active exploitation.

The legacy "90+30" disclosure window—which granted vendors 90 days to patch with an optional 30-day grace period—is no longer considered sufficient for mitigating exposure risks. Shorter fixed deadlines reduce the temporal window during which unpatched flaws can be weaponized against vulnerable software and systems.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Software vendors and enterprise software maintainers should review and streamline their internal vulnerability management and patch development workflows to support accelerated remediation timelines. Organizations should prioritize rapid patch deployment mechanisms to ensure critical updates are applied before technical details are made public.

### Executive Summary
Ontinue security researchers have uncovered details surrounding Lunex, a malware-as-a-service (MaaS) platform associated with the deployment of Psychedelic Stealer. The campaign utilizes compromised Ukrainian websites to target Ukrainian-speaking users through a four-stage attack chain.

Initial entry relies on social engineering schemes featuring fake Cloudflare CAPTCHA and ClickFix-style verification checks to deceive victims into initiating the payload execution chain.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The attack chain begins on compromised websites where victims are presented with a fake CAPTCHA page. This ClickFix-style lure tricks users into completing actions that launch the multi-stage malware delivery process.

Once deployed, the Lunex stealer abuses an AMD driver to terminate or disable security monitoring tools on the endpoint. Following successful defense evasion, the malware targets and exfiltrates stored browser credentials.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Security teams should educate users on recognizing ClickFix-style social engineering tactics and unverified CAPTCHA prompts. Network administrators should monitor endpoints for unusual driver-loading activity or unexpected attempts to disable security monitoring services.

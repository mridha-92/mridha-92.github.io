### Executive Summary
A technical query submitted to Reddit's cybersecurity community details a distributed SMS bombing scenario where attackers target 50 to 100 distinct web and mobile services simultaneously. The attack script targets public registration and login endpoints across various sectors, such as e-commerce and food delivery applications.

By issuing a single "Send OTP" request to each service for the exact same target phone number, the attacker causes the victim to receive 50 to 100 SMS verification messages within seconds. The issue highlights concerns regarding business logic flaws, abuse of unauthenticated endpoints, and the difficulty individual organizations face when detecting cross-service aggregation.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause of this vector is the availability of unauthenticated public endpoints that trigger external SMS dispatches without mechanisms to detect cross-platform coordination. Because the attack relies on distributing single requests across independent applications, cross-service aggregation cannot be tracked or correlated by any single platform.

From the technical impact perspective, individual application rate-limiting controls (such as restricting requests to 1 SMS per minute per phone number) remain unviolated and respected by the attacker. Consequently, single-service defenses are bypassed, resulting in denial-of-service and SMS flooding on the victim's device through aggregated functional abuse.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Defensive and architectural approaches to prevent public endpoints from being weaponized in distributed SMS attacks remain under evaluation by the community, with concrete cross-service solutions not yet confirmed. Recommended practices generally focus on contextual risk scoring, CAPTCHA implementation on public OTP requests, and monitoring request patterns prior to dispatching SMS payloads.

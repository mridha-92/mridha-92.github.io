### Executive Summary
A malicious email containing a suspicious disk image attachment was reportedly delivered to an employee at a large company. Analysis of the attachment revealed a disk image containing a benign executable alongside a malicious dynamic-link library (DLL) designed to hinder reverse-engineering efforts.

The malicious DLL incorporates anti-analysis mechanics specifically crafted to crash disassemblers. When static analysis was attempted, the file triggered an application crash in IDA Pro, preventing straightforward inspection of its underlying code.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The delivery mechanism relies on a disc image file named `INT-Number.20260821152655.IMG`. Inside the archive, two distinct files were identified: `INT-Number.20260821152655.exe`, which is a harmless and well-known executable, and `hdp.dll`, which was flagged as malicious by detection engines.

The root cause of the static analysis failure is a compromised stack frame within `hdp.dll`. The payload features an intentionally malformed or oversized stack structure designed to "poison" the disassembler, causing IDA Pro to implode upon loading the file for inspection.

### Mitigation & IOCs
To mitigate potential exposure, organizations should inspect and restrict incoming email attachments featuring archive and disk image extensions (such as `.IMG` or `.ISO`). Security operations teams should also monitor endpoints for untrusted DLLs being loaded alongside legitimate executables and conduct dynamic analysis in isolated sandbox environments when static disassemblers fail.

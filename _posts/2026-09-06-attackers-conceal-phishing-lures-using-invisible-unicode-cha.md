### Executive Summary

Threat actors are actively leveraging ASCII smuggling techniques within phishing campaigns to bypass traditional email security controls. By embedding invisible Unicode characters into email content, attackers effectively conceal malicious lures from security gateways.

This technique allows malicious emails to bypass automated inspection mechanisms, delivering phishing lures directly to user inboxes. The operational deployment of invisible Unicode characters highlights a growing trend in filter evasion tactics designed to reduce detection rates across enterprise environments.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause of this evasion method is the manipulation of character encoding through ASCII smuggling. Attackers insert specialized invisible Unicode characters into the raw text of phishing emails, rendering the malicious payload or text undetected by automated scanners.

The technical impact includes reduced efficacy of standard email security filters, which may parse or ignore the invisible characters during evaluation. As a result, phishing lures successfully reach end users without triggering standard spam or phishing signatures, increasing the risk of user compromise.

### Mitigation & IOCs

To mitigate this threat, organizations should configure email security tools to detect, inspect, or strip non-printable and invisible Unicode character sequences prior to message delivery. Email gateways should also enforce strict text normalization protocols during content analysis to expose hidden text lures.

### Executive Summary
A security incident involving a Google Workspace user highlights potential session hijacking risks despite existing multi-factor authentication (MFA) controls. A user on an iPhone clicked a link within a phishing email originating from a compromised partner NGO's Google Workspace account. 

The malicious link did not present an authentication prompt, yet an unauthorized actor immediately established a session from an out-of-state location. The incident was contained when the user promptly realized the mistake and performed a manual password reset, which revoked the malicious session.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
* **Root Cause:** The victim received an email from an established contact at an NGO whose Google Workspace account had been previously compromised. Interacting with the embedded link allowed the attacker to bypass or compromise active session credentials without triggering an MFA prompt.
* **Technical Impact:** Within the brief window between the link click and the password reset, the adversary signed into the victim's Google Workspace account from another state, added an unauthorized macOS device to the user's account device list, and requested a data sync.
* **Environment Context:** The impacted organization operates a fully remote, traveling workforce utilizing Google Workspace Business Standard alongside Apple macOS and iOS hardware, operating outside traditional Windows or Microsoft Entra ID environments.

### Mitigation & IOCs
* **Session Termination:** The user initiated an immediate password reset, which successfully severed active session tokens and cut off adversary access.
* **Architecture Recommendations:** Organizations operating non-Windows and non-Entra ID environments should evaluate tailored Identity Threat Detection and Response (ITDR) solutions designed for Google Workspace and macOS assets to detect rapid device enrollments and out-of-state logons.

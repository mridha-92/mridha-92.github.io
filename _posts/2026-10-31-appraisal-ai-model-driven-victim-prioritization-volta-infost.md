### Executive Summary

Talos Intelligence has published details on VOLTA, a self-deleting infostealer malware designed to harvest sensitive user data. The threat targets credentials, session tokens, and cryptocurrency wallets from compromised environments.

The infostealer integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) into its post-compromise workflow. It leverages LLMs to profile victims and automatically rank their most valuable stolen assets for attacker prioritization.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The technical impact of VOLTA includes the unauthorized extraction of user credentials, active browser or application session data, and cryptocurrency wallet information. This compromise exposes affected individuals and organizations to account takeover and direct financial loss.

The malware operates via a self-deleting execution routine to reduce its footprint on infected host systems. Following data exfiltration, LLMs process the stolen information to evaluate the victim's profile and categorize the value of the compromised assets.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Specific technical mitigations were not provided in the source report. Organizations should rely on robust endpoint security controls to detect infostealer activity, monitor for unauthorized session hijack attempts, and audit environment changes associated with self-deleting executables.

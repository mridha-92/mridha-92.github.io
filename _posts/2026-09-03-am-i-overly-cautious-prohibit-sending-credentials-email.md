### Executive Summary
An IT Director for an organization of approximately 100 staff members raised operational security concerns regarding the transmission of system access credentials over email. The organization maintains an explicit policy prohibiting the email transmission of passwords, PINs, API keys, SSH keys, and Azure Enterprise App client secrets due to the prevalence of email account compromise.

The enforcement of this policy frequently encounters resistance from organizational leadership, end users, external colleagues, and third parties. Resistance is primarily driven by the common assumption that corporate email environments are inherently secure.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause of the operational vulnerability is the reliance on email as a secure transport mechanism for secrets. Email systems are frequent targets for compromise, and sending authentication data via email creates a persistent log of sensitive assets in mailboxes, sent folders, and log files.

If an email account is compromised, threat actors gain immediate visibility into all credentials transmitted across historical email threads. The technical impact includes potential unauthorized access to infrastructure and services via exposed passwords, PINs, API keys, SSH keys, and client secrets for Azure Enterprise Apps.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Based on the provided source, the following mitigations are currently implemented:
* Prohibit the transmission of any credentials or access tokens via email, specifically including passwords, PINs, API keys, SSH keys, and Azure Enterprise App client secrets.
* Enforce alternative, secure mechanisms for sharing credentials to prevent exposure during potential email compromise incidents.

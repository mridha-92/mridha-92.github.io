### Executive Summary

A supply-chain threat campaign has targeted at least 21 major websites, including the BBC, Zoom, CNN, Salesforce, and HSBC, by serving malicious JavaScript through trusted third-party providers. The compromised script responses were identified executing within the context of these high-profile platforms.

The malicious code features financial theft capabilities alongside advanced delivery mechanisms. In addition to skimming payment card details and hijacking cryptocurrency wallets, the scripts contain delivery paths associated with the Coruna and DarkSword iPhone exploit kits. Successful exploitation of iPhone devices via this campaign has not been confirmed.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause stems from the poisoning of script responses served by third-party analytics, optimization, and content platforms. Specifically, malicious code was injected and delivered via Optimizely, Kissmetrics, StorifyMe, and minute-ly.

Technically, the injected JavaScript operates even if primary external attacker infrastructure is disrupted. The scripts contain embedded malware designed to execute locally regardless of whether the attacker's external domain is blocked, posing a resilient threat to site visitors through cryptocurrency wallet hijacking, payment card skimming, and potential exploit kit delivery.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Organizations operating websites that integrate third-party services—particularly Optimizely, Kissmetrics, StorifyMe, and minute-ly—should review, isolate, or temporarily disable these scripts pending vendor verification. Security teams should implement strict Subresource Integrity (SRI) controls and continuously audit external JavaScript dependencies.

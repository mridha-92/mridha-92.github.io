### Executive Summary

Threat actors are leveraging ClickFix-like lures to deploy a previously undocumented remote access trojan (RAT) tracked as ChainScript. 

Reported by Blackpoint Adversary Pursuit Group (APG), the campaign uses social engineering lures that disguise the malware as legitimate software, specifically Spotify, Zoom Workplace, and Microsoft Teams, to trick users into compromise.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause stems from social engineering tactics using ClickFix-style prompts that induce targets to execute malicious code. Successful execution delivers the ChainScript RAT, granting threat actors remote control capabilities over targeted host environments.

According to research from Blackpoint APG, ChainScript has appeared under several internal build names, including ComponentTask33, UpdateDigital, HostShared, and OrchidViolet66.

To complicate traditional network blocking and maintain persistent communication with infected hosts, the threat actors utilize the Polygon network to dynamically rotate their command-and-control (C2) infrastructure.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Specific mitigations were not detailed in the provided source material. Organizations should educate users on identifying ClickFix-style prompts and restrict untrusted script execution or unauthorized application downloads mimicking Spotify, Zoom Workplace, or Microsoft Teams.

### Executive Summary

Security operations discussions highlight an ongoing challenge in vulnerability management: prioritizing active, real-world exploitation over high theoretical severity ratings. Analysts report scenarios where vulnerabilities rated CVSS 5.4 with confirmed in-the-wild exploitation on the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog must be prioritized over CVSS 9.1 vulnerabilities that lack public Proof-of-Concept (PoC) code or active threat activity.

While high CVSS scores frequently draw immediate attention from management, security teams emphasize that confirmed active exploitation presents a higher real-world threat. Defense strategies require educating decision-makers on why operational threat context outweighs static vulnerability scoring.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause of misaligned prioritization stems from over-reliance on static CVSS metrics, which measure theoretical severity rather than dynamic risk. A critical vulnerability (e.g., CVSS 9.1) without an available exploit or PoC carries a lower immediate impact than a lower-severity flaw (e.g., CVSS 5.4) that threat actors are actively leveraging in attacks.

Additionally, binary reliance on the official KEV list introduces operational risk, as public KEV catalog updates can lag real-world exploitation by several days. Security practitioners recommend evaluating exploit maturity across distinct tiers—categorized as no known exploit, PoC available, and actively exploited—to account for how rapidly threat levels change once an exploit becomes public.

### Mitigation & IOCs

To improve vulnerability prioritization and risk reduction, security teams should implement the following measures based on the reported intelligence:

* Prioritize remediation of vulnerabilities with confirmed active in-the-wild exploitation or inclusion in the KEV list over unexploited vulnerabilities with high CVSS scores.
* Track exploit maturity as a distinct evaluation tier (none, PoC exists, actively exploited) rather than relying exclusively on CVSS scores or lagging KEV flags.
* Educate stakeholders on threat intelligence metrics to justify remediating lower-CVSS actively exploited flaws ahead of unexploited critical vulnerabilities.

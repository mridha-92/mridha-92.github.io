### Executive Summary
A cyber espionage campaign codenamed Operation QUICSILVER has been detected targeting government and information technology (IT) sectors in Myanmar. Discovered by researchers at Seqrite Labs, the campaign utilizes social engineering tactics to deliver a custom backdoor.

The activity is currently assessed to be driven by a China-nexus threat actor. The campaign relies on graduation ceremony invitation lures to trick targets into compromising their systems.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause of initial compromise is social engineering via lure documents disguised as graduation ceremony invitations. These malicious lures are used to deploy QUICAgent, a backdoor written in the Go programming language.

Once QUICAgent is successfully delivered to a host within the targeted government or IT network, it grants the threat actor persistent remote access, enabling unauthorized espionage operations on the affected infrastructure.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Specific technical mitigations were not provided in the source report. Organizations are advised to maintain strict email security protocols and exercise caution with unsolicited event invitations and attached documents.

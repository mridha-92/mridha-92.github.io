### Executive Summary

A header-only security scan conducted by the Rackcrunch team evaluated 4,688 US small business websites sampled from a public business directory. The non-invasive study examined HTTPS response headers to assess adoption rates of baseline web application security controls.

The results show significant deficiencies in basic header deployment, with 49.7% of the scanned websites failing to meet any of the seven basic security header criteria evaluated. While 21.2% of sites deploy an enforced Content-Security-Policy (CSP), only 8 out of 4,688 sites (0.17%) deploy a CSP that actively restricts script execution. 

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause of missing security configurations is attributed to the maintenance burden of security policies, particularly CSPs, on small business websites that are rarely updated. Many organizations avoid maintaining complex policy rules to prevent site breakage.

From a technical perspective, the majority of enforced CSP headers observed rely solely on framing protections or upgrade directives rather than restricting script sources. Although the absence of security headers does not directly indicate an active vulnerability or compromise, missing baseline headers eliminates a critical, low-cost browser defense layer against client-side risks like Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) and unauthorized framing.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Website administrators and security personnel should review and deploy basic HTTPS response headers across web applications. Key recommendations derived from the source study include:

* Configure and enforce a Content-Security-Policy (CSP) that explicitly restricts script execution sources.
* Deploy standard defensive response headers addressing framing, transport security, and browser behaviors.
* Maintain minimal header configurations suitable for infrequently updated small business websites to ensure baseline security.

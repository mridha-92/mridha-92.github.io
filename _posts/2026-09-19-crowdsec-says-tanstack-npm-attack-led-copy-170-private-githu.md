### Executive Summary
French cybersecurity firm CrowdSec reported on September 18 that an attacker exfiltrated copies of approximately 170 private GitHub repositories on May 22. The breach was enabled by unrevoked access permissions belonging to an employee who had recently departed the company.

The compromise originated from a broader supply chain attack occurring in May that targeted TanStack npm packages. The malicious packages infected the former employee's laptop and harvested credentials, which were subsequently used to gain unauthorized entry to CrowdSec's GitHub environment.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause of the unauthorized access was a combination of a third-party software supply chain attack and delayed identity offboarding. Malicious versions of TanStack npm packages compromised the former employee's laptop and extracted user credentials.

Because CrowdSec failed to promptly revoke the departed employee's GitHub permissions, the attacker was able to authenticate successfully using the stolen credentials. The impact was limited to the exposure and copying of 170 private repositories, with no further lateral movement or infrastructure disruption reported in the source text.

### Mitigation & IOCs
To mitigate similar vectors, organizations should enforce strict access management protocols that guarantee immediate revocation of access rights upon employee termination. Additionally, software development teams should audit third-party dependency pipelines and npm packages for unauthorized or malicious updates.

### Executive Summary

A practitioner on Reddit CyberSec detailed the initial phase of building an AI guardrail platform designed to act as a proxy layer for detecting Large Language Model (LLM) vulnerabilities. The primary focus of this security layer is identifying prompt injection and jailbreak attempts before inputs reach downstream models.

To establish this proxy layer, lightweight open-source security models were evaluated against public datasets hosted on Hugging Face. The preliminary benchmarking highlighted two candidate models: `meta-llama/Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-86M` and `protectai/deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2`.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

*   **Vulnerability Exposure**: Direct and indirect prompt injection alongside jailbreak techniques pose significant operational risks to LLM deployments, necessitating dedicated validation layers to intercept malicious inputs.
*   **Model Limitations & Evaluation Metrics**: While initial evaluation scores favored ProtectAI's `deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2`, official documentation specifies that the model is constrained to the English language and is not officially calibrated for jailbreak detection, despite empirical test results suggesting otherwise.
*   **Evaluation Bias**: The tester identified potential evaluation overlap, noting that many open-source guardrail models are pre-trained or fine-tuned on the same public Hugging Face datasets used for performance benchmarking.

### Mitigation & IOCs

*   Deploy input-filtering proxy architecture incorporating security-focused classification models, such as `meta-llama/Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-86M` or ProtectAI guardrails, to detect prompt injection attempts.
*   Benchmark potential AI guardrail models using proprietary or non-public datasets to avoid pre-training bias and accurately assess multilingual and jailbreak detection capabilities.

### Executive Summary

A user inquiry posted to the Reddit cybersecurity community highlights security concerns regarding administrative permissions and temporary kernel-level anti-cheat drivers requested during the installation and execution of software, specifically referencing the *Marvel Rivals* launcher. The inquiry follows an operating system reinstallation necessitated by a prior supply chain attack.

The report highlights end-user visibility and concern regarding elevated execution privileges and the threat model associated with anti-cheat software in the context of recurring supply chain threats.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The reported technical concern stems from software launchers requesting elevated administrator privileges at boot, alongside anti-cheat implementations that deploy temporary kernel-level drivers. According to the source material, the anti-cheat driver operates at the kernel level during active gameplay and auto-deletes upon application closure.

Running applications with administrative privileges grants the executable full administrative capabilities on the local machine. Kernel-level driver execution expands the system's attack surface to ring-0 operations, creating potential security risks if the software or its supply chain is compromised.

### Mitigation & IOCs

To address risks associated with elevated execution rights and kernel-level drivers, the following general mitigations apply based on the reported technical context:
* Review and audit software requesting User Account Control (UAC) or administrative privileges before granting access.
* Monitor software execution and monitor system changes to verify driver installation and auto-deletion behaviors.
* Maintain strict software supply chain integrity checks to reduce the risk of compromised binaries executing with elevated privileges.

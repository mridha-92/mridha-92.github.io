### Executive Summary
Since April 2026, the National Vulnerability Database (NVD) has modified its operations to score only CVEs under active exploitation and those affecting software used by the U.S. government. All other vulnerability records are added to the NVD without an official NIST severity score.

This operational shift introduces significant risk to organizations relying strictly on NVD scoring for vulnerability management. Unscored vulnerabilities are categorized as "Deferred" in the NVD API and labeled as "Not Scheduled" on the NVD website, causing status mismatches and deprioritization across automated risk management tools.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
Vulnerability scanners and risk engines that rank threats by NVD severity scores push unscored entries to the bottom of prioritization queues. As a result, critical vulnerabilities with functional public exploits may be treated as low priority or overlooked entirely by security teams.

For example, CVE-2026-6584 affects the SuperAGI agent framework as an authorization bypass vulnerability and has a public exploit available. However, due to the lack of an NVD severity score, automated tools relying on NIST scoring push this active threat down the queue. Additionally, integrations hardcoded to check specific website status labels ("Not Scheduled") may completely fail to ingest records flagged as "Deferred" by the API.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Security teams and developers should reconfigure internal integrations and vulnerability tools to evaluate the API `vulnStatus` field directly. Organizations must ingest alternative vulnerability intelligence and severity scoring sources when NIST scores are absent to prevent high-risk vulnerabilities from going unaddressed. Guidance for reading `vulnStatus` and handling unscored CVEs is available via `https://archanacontent.github.io/nvd-cve-api-docs/`.

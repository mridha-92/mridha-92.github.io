### Executive Summary

A industry discussion published on Reddit's r/cybersecurity highlights concerns that red team engagements are increasingly treated as compliance checkboxes rather than drivers of actual security improvement. 

The author notes that while organizations frequently perform red team exercises to satisfy audit requirements and generate reports, many fail to leverage the findings to meaningfully improve their defensive posture. The post advocates for a stronger focus on purple teaming to bridge the gap between offensive testing and defensive capability enhancements.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

* **Root Cause:** Organizations often focus primarily on completing assessment deliverables and fulfilling compliance mandates, neglecting post-assessment analysis, continuous validation, and cross-team collaboration.
* **Technical Impact:** Red team exercises fail to deliver technical value when organizations focus solely on proving attack feasibility without investigating why the attack succeeded, which detection capabilities failed, or how alternative attack paths can be mitigated.
* **Defensive Deficits:** Without structured joint workflows between offensive and defensive teams, security operations centers (SOCs) miss critical opportunities to identify coverage gaps, refine detection logic, and remediate underlying systemic vulnerabilities.

### Mitigation & IOCs

* Implement purple teaming as a long-term, integrated security posture improvement model rather than an isolated assessment exercise.
* Establish formal remediation workflows following offensive testing to analyze root causes, update detection capabilities, and evaluate unexplored attack paths.
* Focus post-assessment efforts on continuous validation and cross-functional collaboration between red and blue teams to ensure security gaps are effectively closed.

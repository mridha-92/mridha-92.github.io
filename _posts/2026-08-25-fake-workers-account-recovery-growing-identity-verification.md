### Executive Summary
Threat actors are increasingly shifting their tactics away from attacking primary login interfaces, focusing instead on targeting identity establishment and account recovery processes. 

By exploiting weaknesses in identity verification during account recovery or onboarding, attackers leverage social engineering and fake worker personas to acquire legitimate system access.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause of this risk is the reliance on weak or unverified identity establishment and recovery workflows, which often lack the stringent controls applied to primary login channels.

The technical impact involves adversaries successfully bypassing standard authentication protections. By abusing identity setup and recovery mechanisms through social engineering, attackers gain legitimate access rights and valid credentials without directly compromising login protocols.

### Mitigation & IOCs
To counter these threat vectors, organizations must enforce stronger identity verification controls across all user onboarding, identity establishment, and account recovery workflows.

### Executive Summary
Security researchers at Hunt.io discovered an open directory staging threat actor tooling and stolen data belonging to two Philippine organizations: a nuclear research agency and a marine engineering company that contracts with the Philippine Navy. 

The compromise is attributed to a suspected Chinese-speaking threat operator, as evidenced by the widespread use of Simplified Chinese within scripts, logs, and staging folder names. Analysis indicates the actor successfully exfiltrated approximately 9 GB of data, with 176 files totaling ~372 MB recovered directly from the exposed staging directory.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The intrusions relied on known, unpatched vulnerabilities and weak configurations rather than zero-day exploits. The nuclear agency was breached via CVE-2023-49105, leveraging an empty signing secret left as default in an ownCloud deployment. The naval contractor was compromised through CVE-2024-28000 in the LiteSpeed Cache WordPress plugin combined with an XML-RPC brute-force attack utilizing the standard `rockyou.txt` wordlist.

Exfiltrated data includes highly sensitive operational and personnel files, specifically reactor core component databases, radiation safety documents, employee PII, and credential stores. Researchers also identified an active, potentially unrelated EtherHiding campaign on the compromised WordPress site, sharing a loader across 174 IP addresses.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Organizations should immediately implement the following mitigation measures:
* Patch all internet-facing collaboration and content management software.
* Assign a secure, non-default signing key on ownCloud instances.
* Disable WordPress XML-RPC functionality if it is not actively required.
* Enforce multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all administrative and privileged accounts.

### Executive Summary
Following recent Synology security concerns and discussions surrounding PHPshells, a whitelist DNS solution has been proposed to simplify outbound traffic protection for Synology NAS devices. The project aims to provide an alternative to complex firewall rule configurations, which often carry a risk of accidentally disconnecting essential services. 

Designed specifically for Synology DSM (tested on DSM 7.4), the whitelist DNS restricts outbound resolution strictly to necessary domains—such as DSM updates, backup infrastructure, time synchronization, certificate renewal, Tailscale, and Microsoft Office services. By failing all unauthorized DNS resolution requests, the service prevents infected devices from resolving untrusted external domains.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
* **Root Cause:** Standard Synology NAS setups allow unrestricted outbound DNS resolution, allowing potential post-exploitation malware or web shells to establish outbound C2 connections or exfiltrate data. Setting up strict firewall isolation rules is often too complex for non-technical administrators.
* **Technical Impact:** The whitelist DNS restricts network requests to pre-approved domains, ensuring that malware outbound "phone home" attempts, payload downloads, and data uploads fail at the DNS resolution phase.
* **Security Limitations:** The whitelist DNS does not protect against malware using direct IP-based communication (bypassing DNS) nor does it prevent the initial exploitation of known vulnerabilities within Synology DSM or hosted applications.

### Mitigation & IOCs
* **Mitigation Strategy:** Configure Synology DSM DNS settings to point to a protective whitelist DNS server to automatically block resolution for unauthorized external domains.
* **Defense in Depth:** Combine whitelist DNS enforcement with routine patch management to remediate known DSM software vulnerabilities and apply firewall rules to restrict direct IP-based outbound connections.

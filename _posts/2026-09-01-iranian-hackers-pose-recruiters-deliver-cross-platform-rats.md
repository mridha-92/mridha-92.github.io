### Executive Summary
The Iranian threat actor known as Nimbus Manticore has been observed utilizing fake recruitment lures and malicious coding tests to deliver cross-platform Remote Access Trojans (RATs). 

According to tracking by cybersecurity firm Kaspersky, this activity involves two previously undocumented malware families. The campaign demonstrates an evolution in the group's toolset, expanding its operational reach to compromise Linux and Apple macOS operating systems alongside traditional targets.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
* **Root Cause:** The initial access vector relies on social engineering, with attackers posing as recruiters and tricking targets into executing malicious code disguised as coding assessments.
* **Technical Impact:** The deployed RATs are authored in Node.js and JavaScript, enabling cross-platform functionality across macOS and Linux environments. Successful exploitation grants remote access capabilities to the threat group, compromising endpoint security and non-Windows systems across targeted organizations.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Organizations should educate staff and developers to exercise caution when receiving unsolicited recruitment communications, particularly when requested to download or execute code snippets for candidate assessments. Organizations should also monitor for unauthorized Node.js executions on developer workstations.

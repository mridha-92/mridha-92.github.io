### Executive Summary

Threat actors are actively abusing npm and its public mirror infrastructure to host malicious static HTML pages. The hosted pages masquerade as legitimate Cloudflare CAPTCHA verification challenges to deceive visiting users.

The primary objective of this campaign is to trick targets into completing fake verification steps, which subsequently redirect visitors to external, attacker-controlled websites.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

**Root Cause:** Threat actors are taking advantage of the public accessibility and trusted reputation of npm and its repository mirrors to host untrusted HTML files without immediate detection.

**Technical Impact:** Users accessing these hosted pages encounter spoofed Cloudflare CAPTCHA interfaces. Upon interaction, the pages execute redirects that forward victim traffic away from trusted registry domains to infrastructure under the control of threat actors, exposing users to potential phishing or follow-on web-based threats.

### Mitigation & IOCs

Specific vendor mitigation guidance was not detailed in the source material. Standard defensive measures include implementing web filtering rules to inspect traffic originating from repository mirrors and educating users to verify suspicious redirection behavior following web verification prompts.

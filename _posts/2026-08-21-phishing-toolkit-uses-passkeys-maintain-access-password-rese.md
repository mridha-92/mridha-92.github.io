### Executive Summary
Security researchers have identified a new phishing toolkit designated as iAuthFlow V2. The toolkit allows threat actors to register an attacker-controlled passkey during a phishing attack to establish persistent access to target accounts.

This persistence mechanism allows adversaries to bypass traditional post-compromise containment measures. Even if a victim subsequently changes their password and security teams revoke all active session tokens, the attacker maintains account access via the newly registered passkey.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The root cause stems from the toolkit abusing account authentication flows to silently or coercively register an adversary-controlled passkey alongside harvested user credentials.

The technical impact affects post-incident recovery controls. Because passkey-based authentication operates independently of traditional passwords, invalidating session cookies and enforcing password resets fails to revoke the attacker's authentication vector.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Specific mitigations were not provided in the source material. Organizations utilizing passkeys should ensure identity management systems log new passkey registrations, notify users of newly added authentication methods, and allow administrators to audit and revoke unauthorized passkeys.

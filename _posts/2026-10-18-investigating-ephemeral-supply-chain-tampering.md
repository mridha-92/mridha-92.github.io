### Executive Summary

Researchers from Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant revealed a supply chain attack targeting software-update infrastructure owned by AOMEI, a vendor that develops Windows backup, disk-cloning, and remote-access software. Between April and July 2026, threat actors repeatedly compromised the company's update servers to deliver malicious payloads to victim endpoints.

The attack resulted in the delivery of malware identified by researchers as CROSSROAD and SHINYROAD. AOMEI was notified by security researchers and reported taking corrective action to remediate its update infrastructure.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause

The root cause of the incident involves unauthorized access to AOMEI's software-update servers, where attackers repeatedly modified configuration files. The threat actors utilized an ephemeral modification technique, keeping malicious configurations active for brief windows lasting only five to ten minutes before restoring legitimate configurations. 

During these active windows, endpoints checking for legitimate software updates were instructed to download malicious files. Because the attackers quickly restored authentic server configurations, the compromise was difficult to detect using standard periodic security audits. Consequently, users downloading legitimate software from official channels still suffered endpoint infections during these transient update windows.

### Mitigation & IOCs

AOMEI has taken corrective measures to secure its update server infrastructure following notification from researchers. Security teams should be aware that remediating update servers does not automatically remove malware from endpoints infected during the active compromise window; impacted host systems must be independently investigated and remediated.

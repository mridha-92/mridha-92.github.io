### Executive Summary
A sophisticated Unified Extensible Firmware Interface (UEFI) bootkit named "BlackX" has been detected targeting diplomatic organizations across Central Asia. Active in early 2026, the cyber espionage campaign specifically singled out diplomatic entities located in Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan.

According to a technical report published by Kaspersky, the threat activity is suspected to be orchestrated by APT41, a China-linked state-sponsored threat group. 

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
The BlackX bootkit operates at the firmware level, enabling it to bypass Secure Boot protections on patched Windows systems that lack updated revocation lists. 

By executing prior to the loading of the operating system, BlackX establishes deep persistence on compromised endpoints. This allows threat actors to maintain long-term access while evading standard OS-level security controls and monitoring tools.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Organizations should ensure that UEFI Secure Boot revocation lists (DBX) are regularly updated alongside standard operating system patches to prevent revoked bootloaders from bypassing Secure Boot defenses. Firmware integrity monitoring should also be implemented where feasible.

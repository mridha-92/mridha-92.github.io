### Executive Summary
A community discussion published on the r/cybersecurity forum evaluated the efficacy of traditional cybersecurity awareness training compared to procedural, process-aligned training. The inquiry proposes moving away from abstract or uncommon threat concepts toward direct end-user operational processes.

The discussion highlights specific scenarios, such as helpdesk impersonation and password reset workflows, advocating that training should directly mirror established organizational policies and be routinely validated through simulations.

### Technical Impact & Root Cause
Focusing awareness programs on broad conceptual threats (such as "juice jacking") can create a gap in an organization's defense posture, leaving personnel unprepared for direct social engineering vectors like phone-based helpdesk impersonation.

The underlying issue stems from training programs that do not align end-user instruction with formal operational procedures. Without clear, repeatable steps for identity verification and password resets, employees lack actionable guidelines during live security events.

### Mitigation & IOCs
Recommended procedural mitigations based on the source material include:
* Aligning security awareness training directly with organizational policies, standards, and workflow procedures.
* Establishing explicit interaction procedures, such as defining verified communication channels for IT helpdesk staff during password resets.
* Conducting targeted simulations to validate that end users understand and follow defined operational processes.
